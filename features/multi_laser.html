Multi-laser: scanner array and laser assignment

Large LPBF machines have several lasers, each with its own scanner that reaches only part of the plate, its scan field. The buildstyle describes the scanners as a grid, the scanner array, and gives every path of a layer to one laser. Where fields overlap, it shares the work so that all lasers finish at about the same time: the layer is only done when the slowest laser is done.

In Advanced Toolpath Utility

    Try it yourself

    Make the profile you edit the platform profile first, otherwise the platform parameters have no effect. Then open it, expand Platform Parameter › Scanner array and describe your machine: number of scanners, their spacing, the size of one scan field and the platform. Under Laser assignment switch Enable laser assignment on, choose a strategy and rebuild.

    The Exposure Preview then draws every laser in its own colour. In the demo each of the four lasers reaches the whole plate, so the load balancer is free to give every part to any laser: the busiest one ends up with 28 % of the hatch length instead of 100 %, close to the ideal quarter, so the layer takes a little over a quarter of the time of one laser.

    The defaults describe a 250 × 250 mm platform with 2 × 2 scanners; the demo sets an array for its 60 × 60 mm plate with thirteen parts (parts/laser_parts.stl, generated by tools/make_parts.py) and makes its "Docs demo" profile the platform profile for the recording.

    One large part: field split

      When a single part is larger than one scan field, whole parts cannot be shared. The Field split strategy cuts the hatch vectors at the field borders instead. Here four 32 mm fields, 24 mm apart, cover a 40 × 40 mm plate (parts/laser_plate.stl); in the 8 mm overlaps the vectors are shared between neighbouring lasers, and the slowest laser gets 31 % of the hatch.

      Scanner array

      The scanners sit on a regular grid, centred on Array center X/Y, Scanner spacing apart. Every scanner reaches a rectangle of Scan field width × height around itself. With a spacing smaller than the field, neighbouring fields overlap and the buildstyle can choose the laser there. The lasers are numbered row by row, starting at the bottom left, and each gets a colour spread evenly around the colour wheel, so any number of lasers stays easy to tell apart.

      Field split

      Every hatch vector is cut at the borders of the scan fields. A piece inside one field goes to that laser; a piece in an overlap goes to the candidate whose work would be smallest after taking it, measured as estimated time (jumps, marking, delays from Laser timing) or as marked length. Ties go to the lower laser number, so the result is the same on every run. Contours are never cut: a whole border goes to one laser. On the laser plate the slowest laser gets about a third of the hatch, so the layer takes about a third of the time of one laser.

      Swimlanes

      The swimlane strategies cut the platform into bands of Swimlane width across the gas flow and expose them one after the other, the band furthest downstream first, so fumes and spatter are blown onto areas that are already done. Inside a band the paths go to the lasers whose field contains their centre, again with the smallest work first; the work counter starts empty in every band.

      • Swimlane never cuts a path: a whole hatch goes into the band of its centre.
      • Swimlane + clipping cuts the hatch vectors at the band borders, so no piece lies in two bands. With Split contours at swimlanes the contours are cut as well.
      • Swimlane + reassign never cuts a vector: each one goes into the band of its middle point.

      The swimlane strategies balance whole paths, so they are made for many parts: every part (or the piece of it in one band) is exposed by one laser, as in the video above. Because the work counter starts empty in every band, a band as wide as the platform shares all parts in one go; narrower bands balance each band on its own.

      The balancer works greedily in exposure order and does not look ahead. Parts that only one laser can reach count against that laser only when their turn comes, so with small scan fields the shared parts may already have gone to that laser before. The more of the plate every laser reaches, the closer the result gets to an even split: in Try it, make the scan fields of the thirteen parts smaller and watch the slowest laser grow. On a single plate, the hatch of one band is one path and goes to one laser; use Field split there.

      Layer time

      After the assignment the power optimization runs separately for every laser, then the buildstyle estimates the time of every laser in the final order. The layer time is the time of the slowest laser. It is stored with the build (layer_built_time_estimation_ms_<n> per layer, built_time_estimation_ms in total), and the export writes the total to export_info.json.

        How to try a code change
        1. Work on a copy or a git branch of the buildstyle project.
        2. Open the project folder in an editor such as VS Code. ATU writes jsconfig.json and type declarations into the folder, so the editor can offer code completion.
        3. Make the edit, save, compile the project in ATU and rebuild the exposures.
        4. Check the result in the Exposure Preview or the 3D View, like in the video above.
