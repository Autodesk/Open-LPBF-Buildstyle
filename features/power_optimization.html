Power optimization

On a short hatch vector the laser comes back to its last line after a fraction of a millisecond, while the metal there is still hot. With full power the heat builds up: the melt pool grows, and pores, swollen edges and spatter follow. The power optimization gives every hatch vector its own power: less on short vectors, full power on long ones. Speed and hatch distance stay the same.

In Advanced Toolpath Utility

    Try it yourself

    Open the parameter profile of your part (Open Profile in the Exposure Preview), expand Hatching › Volume / hull hatching and set Power optimization to on. Rebuild and look at the short vectors of your part.

    To see the power, the demo uses its own colour mode of the Exposure Preview: Configure… next to Color mode, a colormap of the point attribute power with the fixed range 150 … 280 W. Before the change none of the hatch vectors of layer 50 shows the low-power colours; after the rebuild the vectors near the tips do. The demo part parts/power_wedge.stl is generated by tools/make_parts.py.

    Deep dive

    A closer look at how the power optimization works, from the heat build-up on short vectors to the power it gives every hatch vector.

    Try it

    Short vectors, short return time

    The power wedge is a right triangle, 36 × 18 mm. Its hatch vectors get shorter towards the tips, down to a few hundredths of a millimetre. What matters for the heat is the return time: how long the laser needs until it is back next to the start of a vector. The buildstyle counts marking time (length ÷ speed), the mark delay, one jump of a hatch distance and the jump delay. At 1200 mm/s a 0.5 mm vector returns after 0.49 ms, a 10 mm vector after 8.4 ms.

    From time to power

    The dialog asks for two vector lengths, because lengths are easier to imagine than times. The code turns them into the two return times and interpolates the power linearly in between. Up to 0.5 mm the vectors get the minimum power, from 10 mm on the maximum. Because the return time grows linearly with the length, the Power curve is a straight ramp between the two lengths.

    Segment splitting

    Without splitting a vector gets one power from its own length. That is an estimate: in a meander the laser comes back right next to the end where it turned, but only after two vector lengths at the far end. Switch on Segment splitting: every vector is cut into segments of at most the segment length, and each segment gets the power of its real return time, from the moment the previous line passed the closest point until this segment is exposed. In the wedge the colour now changes along every line: the turning ends get less power, the far ends more. If the previous line is more than 1.5 hatch distances away (no hot neighbour), the segment gets the maximum power.

    Merge segments with equal power joins neighbouring segments of the same power back into one vector. The powers are rounded to 0.1 W, so long stretches with the minimum or maximum power become single vectors again. Switch it off and compare the number of segments below the wedge. A shorter segment length follows the return time more closely but gives more data.

    One timeline per laser

    The power is set after the build order and the laser assignment, so it follows the vectors in the order the lasers really scan them. All hatch blocks of one vector type on the same laser form one timeline, across islands and, with the Global build order, across parts. With segment splitting a segment can therefore find its hot neighbour in the block the laser exposed just before, for example where the laser assignment cut one block into runs on lasers 1, 2 and 1 again. Without splitting the timeline changes nothing: every vector keeps the power of its own length.

    The splitting in this view treats the wedge as one meander block in line order, at the volume hatch speed of 1200 mm/s. If you use jump delay scaling, the short jump of one hatch distance also gets the shorter jump delay, so the return times shrink a little.

    Set the maximum power to the power of the vector type in the Scanning group (280 W for the volume hatch). The buildstyle does not check this; a higher maximum would raise the power of long vectors.
