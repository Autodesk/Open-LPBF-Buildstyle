Fill contours

Fill contours are concentric rings between the borders and the hatch. They give a smooth transition from the borders to the hatch, and in thin regions they replace the hatch completely: a rib that is narrower than the rings need is filled with rings only.

In Advanced Toolpath Utility

    Try it yourself

    Open the parameter profile of your part (Open Profile in the Exposure Preview), tick Fill contours, expand it and set Number of fill contours. Rebuild and look at a thin feature in the Exposure Preview: the orange rings take over where the hatch used to be.

    In the demo, ATU reports five BSID 300 rings with 323.31 mm in total on layer z = 1.02 mm. The demo part parts/contour_plate.stl (24 × 10 mm with a chamfer, a notch and a 1 mm tab) is generated by tools/make_parts.py.

    Rings after the borders

    The fill contours start at the innermost border: the first border at 0.06 mm when there are no following borders. Ring j then runs at innermost border + first offset + j · following offset. With five rings 0.1 mm apart they sit at 0.16, 0.26, 0.36, 0.46 and 0.56 mm, and the hatch starts at 0.61 mm. Add following borders with the third slider: the fill rings move inwards with them.

    Thin regions without hatch

    Open the Thin tab view. The tab is 1 mm wide, so each side has 0.5 mm. Four of the five rings fit, the fifth one and the hatch do not. The tab is filled with orange rings only, as in the video, where the Exposure Preview shows the hatch ending at x = 17.68 mm and the tab beginning at x = 18 mm. With fewer rings a narrow hatch strip comes back in the middle of the tab.

    In the Section view, see how much the tracks overlap: 0.1 mm spacing and a 0.12 mm track overlap by only 20 µm, so the rings are just closed.

    Scan direction

    Open the Direction view and move the layer slider. Two switches decide the direction of each ring, and a ring is reversed when exactly one of them asks for it: alternate every layer reverses all rings on layers 2, 4, 6 …, alternate every contour reverses rings 2, 4, 6 … of the group. With both on (the default for fill contours) neighbouring rings always run in opposite directions, and the whole pattern swaps from layer to layer. The rings are counted per group, so the first fill ring is ring 1 even when following borders come before it. The ring spacing is exaggerated in this view so that the arrows can be read.

    Drawn to scale from the outline of the demo plate. The rings are iso-lines of the distance to the outline on a 0.025 mm grid, the hatch is drawn at a fixed 45° and the melt tracks in the section are simplified half ellipses.

      How to try a code change
      1. Work on a copy or a git branch of the buildstyle project.
      2. Open the project folder in an editor such as VS Code. ATU writes jsconfig.json and type declarations into the folder, so the editor can offer code completion.
      3. Make the edit, save, compile the project in ATU and rebuild the exposures.
      4. Check the result in the Exposure Preview or the 3D View, like in the video above.
