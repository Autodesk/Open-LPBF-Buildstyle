Upskin (one layer)

The upskin is the top surface of a part: the area of a layer that the next layer does not cover. It gets its own hatch with a smaller hatch distance and its own laser settings, for a denser and smoother top. This buildstyle compares only with the next layer, so each top surface gets one layer of upskin.

In Advanced Toolpath Utility

    Try it yourself

    Open the parameter profile of your part (Open Profile in the Exposure Preview) and tick Upskin (one layer). Rebuild, then step through the layers in the Exposure Preview to the last layer below a top surface: the top area is hatched in yellow.

    In the demo, ATU finds upskin on the layers z = 1.98, 3.99 and 6.00 mm and none on 2.01 mm. On the top layer all hatch is upskin (951.1 mm). The demo part parts/step_block.stl (24 × 10 mm, steps 2, 4 and 6 mm high) is generated by tools/make_parts.py.

    Which area is upskin?

    An area is upskin if the layer above does not cover it. The step block has steps with tops at 2, 4 and 6 mm. In the Section view, drag the layer slider to z = 1.98 mm: the next layer, 2.01 mm, is above the lowest step, so that whole step is upskin. One layer higher there is no upskin at all, and the next upskin comes on the last layer of the middle step, at 3.99 mm.

    The comparison covers only one layer. Thicker top skins (several layers of upskin) are not part of this buildstyle.

    Overlap with the volume

    Open the Top view. The upskin is grown by the overlap (0.1 mm) into the neighbouring volume and both are clipped to the hatch area. The two hatches overlap a little instead of leaving a seam. The lengths at the bottom are area ÷ hatch distance: 977 mm of upskin and 1544 mm of volume hatch at z = 1.98 mm, the same as the 976.78 mm and 1544.26 mm that ATU reports in the video.

    Minimum width and sloped tops

    On a sloped top every layer sticks out beyond the next one by only t / tan(angle): 52 µm for a 30° slope with 30 µm layers. Every layer would get a narrow strip of very short upskin vectors. The minimum width (0.1 mm) removes such slivers. Try it in the Sloped top view: below about 17° the strips are wide enough and stay.

    Section and top view are drawn to scale, except that the current layer is drawn thicker than 30 µm so it stays visible. In the top view the hatch lines are drawn wider apart than the real 0.1 and 0.08 mm; the lengths are computed with the real distances. The sloped top is a sketch with exaggerated layer height.

      How to try a code change
      1. Work on a copy or a git branch of the buildstyle project.
      2. Open the project folder in an editor such as VS Code. ATU writes jsconfig.json and type declarations into the folder, so the editor can offer code completion.
      3. Make the edit, save, compile the project in ATU and rebuild the exposures.
      4. Check the result in the Exposure Preview or the 3D View, like in the video above.
