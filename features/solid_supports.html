Solid supports

Solid supports are support areas below overhangs, such as pillars or blocks. They only have to hold the part and lead the heat into the build plate, so they get a plain, coarse hatch without any border. An optional inset keeps the hatch away from the support outline.

In Advanced Toolpath Utility

    Try it yourself

    Load a part together with its supports (in the input file manager, mark the support file as support). In the parameter profile, expand Solid supports, type an inset and choose a pattern under Support hatching. Rebuild and zoom into a support in the Exposure Preview.

    In the demo, ATU reports 6 BSID 710 hatches with 269.88 mm on z = 2.01 mm before, and 30 rings with 120 mm after the change (0.5 mm inset, Offset). The demo parts parts/support_table.stl and parts/solid_supports.stl are generated by tools/make_parts.py.

    Just a hatch

    Six 3 × 3 mm pillars carry the demo table. Every pillar is filled with meander lines 0.2 mm apart, right up to its outline: about 45 mm of lines per pillar and 270 mm for all six, which matches the 269.88 mm ATU reports. There is no border, because the surface of a support does not matter.

    Inset

    With an inset the area is shrunk before it is hatched. With 0.5 mm, each pillar is hatched on only 2 × 2 mm. The support then touches the part on a smaller area and breaks off more easily.

    Meander or offset

    Support areas offer two patterns. Meander draws parallel lines that turn at the edge. Offset draws rings parallel to the outline: the first ring half a hatch distance inside, then one ring every hatch distance. For the 2 × 2 mm area that gives five rings and 20 mm per pillar. With 0.5 mm inset and Offset, the lines shrink to 120 mm for all six pillars, exactly as in the video.

    The support hatch has the same sub-groups as the part hatches. Its rotation turns the meander by 67° per layer (move the layer slider). For Offset, expose from inside to outside reverses the ring order: the dark ring is exposed first, the lighter ones follow.

    Drawn to scale from the demo pillars. The length shown for the meander is area ÷ hatch distance, the connections at the turns are not counted.

      How to try a code change
      1. Work on a copy or a git branch of the buildstyle project.
      2. Open the project folder in an editor such as VS Code. ATU writes jsconfig.json and type declarations into the folder, so the editor can offer code completion.
      3. Make the edit, save, compile the project in ATU and rebuild the exposures.
      4. Check the result in the Exposure Preview or the 3D View, like in the video above.
