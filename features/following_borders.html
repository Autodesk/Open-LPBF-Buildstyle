Following borders

Following borders are extra closed rings just inside the first border. Together they form a thicker, denser outer wall. Every ring is the outline shifted inwards by its own distance, and the hatch moves inwards with the innermost ring.

In Advanced Toolpath Utility

    Try it yourself

    Open the parameter profile of your part (Open Profile in the Exposure Preview), tick Following borders, expand it and set Number of borders. Rebuild and zoom into an edge of the Exposure Preview: the new red rings sit between the first border and the hatch.

    In the demo, ATU reports three BSID 200 rings with 204.62 mm in total on layer z = 1.02 mm, and the hatch starts 0.35 mm inside the outline. The demo part parts/contour_plate.stl (24 × 10 mm with a chamfer, a notch and a 1 mm tab) is generated by tools/make_parts.py.

    Rings measured from the outline

    Ring i runs at beam compensation + first offset + (i − 1) · following offset inside the outline. With the defaults (0.06 mm, 0.08 mm, 0.08 mm) three rings sit at 0.14, 0.22 and 0.30 mm. Each ring is a new offset of the outline, not of the previous ring, so changing one value never shifts the others by surprise. Move First offset and all rings shift together; move Following offset and only the gaps between them change.

    The hatch starts hatch offset (0.05 mm) inside the innermost ring: 0.35 mm with three rings instead of 0.11 mm without. In the video ATU shows exactly that: the green hatch draws back from the outline.

    Why the spacing is smaller than the track

    The melt track is about 0.12 mm wide, the default spacing 0.08 mm. Neighbouring rings therefore overlap by about 40 µm and leave no powder unmelted between them. Open the Section view and drag the following offset above 0.12 mm: a gap appears.

    Scan direction

    Open the Direction view and move the layer slider. A ring is reversed when exactly one of two switches asks for it: alternate every layer (on by default) reverses all rings on layers 2, 4, 6 …, alternate every contour (off by default) reverses rings 2, 4, 6 … With the defaults all following borders run the same way and swap direction from layer to layer. Switch both on and neighbouring rings run in opposite directions. The ring spacing is exaggerated in this view so that the arrows can be read.

    Corners and thin regions

    At the chamfer the rings follow the outline at constant distance. In the 1 mm Thin tab the three rings on each side leave a hatch strip of only 0.3 mm. Add more rings and they meet in the middle: there is no room for a hatch any more, the inner rings simply stop where the tab is too narrow.

    Drawn to scale from the outline of the demo plate. The rings are iso-lines of the distance to the outline on a 0.025 mm grid, the hatch is drawn at a fixed 45° and the melt tracks in the section are simplified half ellipses.

      How to try a code change
      1. Work on a copy or a git branch of the buildstyle project.
      2. Open the project folder in an editor such as VS Code. ATU writes jsconfig.json and type declarations into the folder, so the editor can offer code completion.
      3. Make the edit, save, compile the project in ATU and rebuild the exposures.
      4. Check the result in the Exposure Preview or the 3D View, like in the video above.
