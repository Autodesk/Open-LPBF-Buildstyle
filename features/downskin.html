Multi-layer overhang (downskin)

Areas without enough solid material below are exposed with downskin settings: less energy, so the melt pool does not sink into the loose powder underneath.

In Advanced Toolpath Utility

    Try it yourself

    Open the parameter profile of your part (Open Profile in the Exposure Preview), set Multi-layer overhang (downskin) to on and adjust the surface angle and reference layers. Rebuild, then step through the layers: downskin vectors are drawn in blue in the Exposure Preview and in the 3D View.

    What is downskin?

    A downskin (down-facing surface, overhang) is the area of a layer that lies above loose powder instead of solid metal. Powder conducts heat much worse than solid metal, so the heat stays in the melt pool. With normal parameters the melt pool becomes too large, sinks into the powder below and leaves dross (stuck powder) and a rough bottom surface. Downskin areas are therefore exposed with less energy.

    The surface angle is the angle between the part surface and the build plate. Walls steeper than the "self-supporting" angle (often about 45°) can be built without downskin parameters. Move the slider: below 30° only the flat shelf is downskin, above 60° all three undersides are.

    How the buildstyle finds it

    Multi-layer means the detection looks at several layers below (reference layers), not only at the directly preceding one. A flat overhang shifts only a little from layer to layer; comparing with more layers detects such areas more reliably. Switch to Layers to see it: the flat shelf becomes downskin in its first layers, a slope flatter than the limit leaves a narrow downskin strip along its edge in every layer.

    The layer view is a simplified model with very thick layers so you can see them. The buildstyle works with the real layer thickness (30 µm in the demo), where the strip along the 30° slope is only about a tenth of a millimetre wide (see the zoomed preview in the video).

    Narrow downskin slivers below the minimum width are removed with a morphological opening, and the result is clipped to the island so the downskin never reaches outside the part outline. The downskin hatch then reaches overlap mm into the volume so both hatches bond.

    Minimum width, overlap and splitting in the layer view

    In the Layers view, raise the minimum width to 2.5 mm: the strips along the 30° slope (about 2.2 mm wide in this exaggerated model) disappear and are hatched as volume again, while the 5 to 7 mm wide strip under the flat shelf stays. Raise the overlap: the light blue band shows how far the downskin hatch reaches into the volume. It only grows into the hatch area, never into the border or fill contour.

    The border and the fill contour run around the whole layer, so they also cross the downskin area. With border splitting and fill contour splitting (both on by default) the part of the ring inside the downskin becomes its own vector type, the downskin border (BSID 101, 201) or the downskin fill contour (BSID 301), with lower power. Switch them off and the whole ring keeps the normal type. Both switches only work while the downskin group is on.

    Like the layers, the bands of the border and fill contour are drawn much wider than they are. The overlap and minimum width are applied at their real value in mm, so with the exaggerated layers they have to be set larger than the defaults to show an effect.

    Laser settings (group Scanning)

    Every downskin vector type is its normal type's BSID + 1, with less power:

    Vector typeBSIDPowerSpeed
    Volume hatch500280 W1200 mm/s
    Downskin hatch501120 W1000 mm/s
    First border100150 W600 mm/s
    First border (downskin)101100 W900 mm/s
    Fill contours300200 W1000 mm/s
    Fill contours (downskin)301110 W1000 mm/s

    With 30 µm layers the volume hatch puts about 78 J/mm³ into the powder (E = P / (v · h · t) = 280 / (1200 · 0.1 · 0.03)), the downskin hatch about 50 J/mm³ (120 / (1000 · 0.08 · 0.03)). The downskin hatch (scan order 30) is exposed before the volume hatch (40).

      How to try a code change
      1. Work on a copy or a git branch of the buildstyle project.
